## Project Concept

A cassette is a medium marked by time. Placing a tape into a player, closing the lid, watching the reels turn slowly, and hearing the sound emerge all carry a sense of warmth, healing, and slightly blurred memory. This zine uses the cassette as a metaphor for a photo book, transforming private images from individual photographs into a rhythmic reading experience.

I made the book as a "cassette," recording everyday and special memories into a physical object. It is small enough to hold and browse like a piece of time. When these "tapes" are collected and stacked, fragments of life gradually form an archive. Pulling out one box on an afternoon becomes a way to remember slowly.

## Images

The origin of this zine goes back to a journey across half the globe during university.

I still think of those ten days surrounded by endless grass. It was an unnamed sense of awe: tiny humans entering the earth of all living things. We name each piece of land as if naming grants us mastery over the world. On the grasslands, however, I was moved to tears by herds of wildebeest, elephants, and giraffes wandering freely. They belong to that land and that world; humans become the intruders. They approach and leave slowly and silently. Nature entered my mind with unusual force for the first time, reshaping how I understood life and the world.

The images were taken during a trip across the Tanzanian grasslands. I organized them into three narrative groups: people, animals, and grassland. Through these themes, I tried to reconstruct my memory of that distant and open place.

## Making

The work uses everyday materials such as A4 paper, recycled paper, and rice paper, and the whole process was completed with a home printer. This method keeps the threshold low and allows recording to happen within daily life.

I wanted the publication to begin from the act of making rather than waiting for professional conditions. The process starts by printing photographs, then folding, sewing, and binding them by hand. The simple craft returns attention to recording, organizing, and creating, while preserving the DIY spirit of the zine.
